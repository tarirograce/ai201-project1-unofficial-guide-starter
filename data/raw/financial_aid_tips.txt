Northgate University — Unofficial Financial Aid and Money Tips

Real advice from students on surviving college finances.

Financial aid basics: file the FAFSA as early as possible, ideally in October when it opens. Aid is partly first-come, first-served, so late filers miss out on certain grants. Renew it every single year — many students lose aid simply by forgetting to refile.

Scholarships: the financial aid office maintains a campus scholarship database that most students never check. There are small departmental scholarships, five hundred to two thousand dollars, that get almost no applicants because people do not know about them. Apply to several; the small ones add up. The deadline for most internal scholarships is in February.

Work-study and jobs: work-study jobs are the most flexible because supervisors understand your class schedule. The library, the dining halls, and front desk jobs in the dorms are popular and not too demanding — you can often study during slow shifts. Non-work-study campus jobs pay the same and anyone can apply.

Saving money: buy used or rent textbooks, and check the library reserves before buying anything. Many courses keep a copy on reserve you can borrow for free. The student discount on software and laptops through the campus store is real and substantial. Cook in the dorm kitchen instead of eating out.

Emergencies: the Dean of Students office has a small emergency fund for students facing sudden financial hardship — it is confidential and you do not have to repay it. Almost no one knows it exists.
