Northgate University — Internship and Career Advice (Unofficial)

How students actually land internships and jobs.

Start early: the biggest mistake is waiting until senior year. Summer internship recruiting for many fields happens in the fall of the year before — sometimes a full year ahead. Tech and finance recruit earliest, often in September and October.

The Career Center: it is underused and genuinely helpful. Book a free resume review before applying anywhere. They run mock interviews, and the staff have connections to employers who specifically recruit Northgate students. The career fair each October and February is where many students get their first interviews — go even as a first-year just to practice talking to recruiters.

Networking that works: professors often know about research positions and industry contacts, so go to office hours and express interest. Alumni are surprisingly willing to help current students; the alumni networking platform lets you message graduates in your field. Informational interviews — a fifteen minute chat to learn about someone's job — lead to opportunities more often than cold applications.

Building a resume with no experience: campus jobs, club leadership, and class projects all count. A personal project you can talk about is worth more than a generic resume line. Research with a professor is one of the strongest things you can do, and many professors take first and second-year students if you simply ask.

Applications: tailor your resume to each posting, apply to many, and do not get discouraged by rejections — they are normal. Keep a spreadsheet tracking where and when you applied.
