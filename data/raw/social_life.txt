Northgate University — Social Life and Making Friends (Unofficial)

How to build a social life at Northgate, especially as a newcomer.

The first few weeks: this is the easiest time to make friends because everyone is looking for them. Say yes to things even when you are tired. Leave your dorm door open when you are around — it is an old trick that genuinely works. Eat meals in the dining hall rather than taking food back to your room.

Where friendships form: clubs are the most reliable place to meet people who share your interests. Floor mates become friends by default, so go to the dorm events your RA organizes even if they seem cheesy. Study groups for hard classes turn into friend groups surprisingly often.

Weekend life: there is more to do than parties. The student programming board hosts free events almost every weekend — movie nights, comedy shows, concerts, trivia. The nearby downtown has cafes, a cheap movie theater, and live music. The Outdoors Club trips are a great alcohol-free way to spend a weekend.

If you feel isolated: this is extremely common and almost never talked about. Most students feel lonely at some point in the first semester. It gets better, usually by the second semester once routines form. Counseling services run friendship and social-skills workshops, and there are affinity groups for almost every background and identity.

Long-term: the friends you make are often not the ones from the very first week. Stay open as your interests evolve. Quality over quantity — a few close friends beats a large shallow network.
